Class 6 notes — Genetics: Mendel's first law (fictional example for the demo)

A gene is a segment of DNA containing the information for a trait. Alleles are different versions of the same gene.

A homozygous individual has two identical alleles (AA or aa). A heterozygous individual has two different alleles (Aa).

The dominant allele is expressed even with one copy. The recessive allele is expressed only with two copies (aa).

Genotype is the combination of alleles. Phenotype is the trait that is expressed.

In a cross between two heterozygous individuals (Aa x Aa), the genotype ratio is 1 AA : 2 Aa : 1 aa and the phenotype ratio is 3 dominant to 1 recessive.

Exam tip: 3 to 1 is the expected ratio, not a guarantee when only a few offspring are born.
