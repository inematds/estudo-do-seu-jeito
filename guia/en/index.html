Study your way (Estudo do seu jeito) · free kit for educators

Help your child learn in the way that works for them, even if you don't know the subject

They used to do well, but now they're struggling in one subject. You want to help, but you can barely remember the material. With the Study your way formula, AI teaches you first and helps you build a guide that suits your child. The kit makes it clear which study method is working.

What you get How to get started
In a nutshell

When your child (or a student) starts struggling, the person trying to help often doesn't know the subject or the right way to explain it. Study your way is a free kit with an 8-step formula. AI teaches you first, then builds a study guide suited to the student, with diagrams, real-life examples, and questions drawn only from class materials. It also helps you test which study method works, without hiding anything. All you need is an AI chat. To do more, use Claude Code or Codex.

The story

A mother who isn't a programmer, five hours from her daughter

The formula came from a true story. We've changed the names.

  1. The student who always got A's starts struggling
    Clara went to study marine biology at a university five hours from home. There was a lot to learn in a short time, the lessons were text-heavy slides, and in her subject, missing one question on a learning objective meant losing the entire objective. She learned by seeing and asking questions, but ended up with a D.
  2. Her mother studies first
    Lúcia has four children and has run a marketing agency with her husband from home for 21 years. She took what Clara had got wrong on the test and studied with Claude for two to three hours a day so she could explain it. Clara thought her mother "knew biology."
  3. A guide tailored to her daughter, in one day
    Lúcia is a designer and knew Clara learns by seeing. In four sentences, she asked Claude for a study guide organized around how the teacher assesses students and based on class materials. Then she asked it to turn the guide into a webpage. By that same day, she had something to show.
  4. New, checked materials every week
    Clara photographed her notes after each lesson. The guide was updated and checked against the textbook. At one point, the AI warned them that a guide Clara had filled in contained wrong answers.
  5. The result
    Her grade rose by more than 10 points, from a D to nearly 80. She got every learning objective she'd missed right on the next test. Clara, who had been against AI, came to see it as a way to learn, not to cut corners.

These figures come from the story; they were not measured by this kit. The example included with the kit uses fictional data and says so on screen.

The formula

The 8 steps of Study your way

It's what Lúcia did, put in order. The kit marks each step as done or not based on what you record.

1. You learn first

AI explains the topic your child got wrong, with examples and common traps, then checks that you understand.

2. How the teacher assesses

How the subject is divided up and what costs the student marks.

3. How the student learns

By seeing, hearing, and speaking, using concrete examples, or writing. More than one way may work.

4. What the student got wrong

The most recent test shows where to start.

5. Plan before building

You tell AI everything; it asks what it doesn't know and offers options. At the end, you get a summary of the plan.

6. A guide based only on the materials

Each learning objective has a reading, diagram, real-life example, common traps, and questions that cite a sentence from the materials.

7. Repeat every week

Photos of the student's notes are added, the guide is updated, and anything they wrote down incorrectly is flagged.

8. The student carries on independently

The end goal is for you to step back: the student studies, records their progress, and manages their own guide.

How it works

Who writes, who checks, without jargon

✍️ AI writes

The chat AI, Claude Code, or Codex reads the photos and writes explanations and questions. That's what AI is good at.

🔎 The kit checks

Like a teacher saying, "show me where it is in the book": every question has to include a sentence from the materials. The kit looks for that sentence; if it isn't there, the question is left out.

📒 The study log

Before studying, the student records which method they will use and why. Then they record the result. Nothing is deleted, so you can see the whole journey.

🧪 An open test of study methods

No one knows in advance which study method works for the student. The kit alternates methods that suit how they learn with the usual "reread and highlight" approach for comparison. It only calls a method "ahead" when there are at least 3 tested objectives for each method and a clear difference. Before that, it says "not enough data."

⚠️ What the kit doesn't promise

  • It doesn't promise a particular grade; it helps the student study better.
  • It checks whether a sentence appears in the materials, not whether the materials are correct. That's why the textbook is included as a source.
  • The scoreboard is an honest clue, not a scientific experiment: different learning objectives have different levels of difficulty.

How to get started

Start with the plan, not the guide

This is where things often go wrong: skip the plan, and you'll have to redo everything.

1. Open your AI

The Claude chat (or another AI chat) is enough. If you use Claude Code or Codex, open it in any folder.

2. Paste the prompt and attach the materials

Photos of the notes, the teacher's guide, and the marked test.

3. Answer its questions

It asks what it doesn't know and offers options. You choose, and only then does it build the guide.

Read the "Study your way" kit at https://github.com/inematds/estudo-do-seu-jeito (the README explains the 8-step formula and includes ready-to-use prompts).
I want to create a study guide for [who], who is studying [subject and level]. I'll attach the materials: notes, the teacher's guide, and the most recent marked test.
1. Don't build anything yet. First, ask me what you need to know: how the teacher assesses students, how [who] learns, and what they got wrong.
2. Give me 2 or 3 options for organizing the guide, with the pros and cons of each.
3. Fixed rule: all content and questions must come only from the materials I provide, with a sentence that supports each answer. If anything is missing, ask me.
4. Once I choose, show me a summary of the plan and wait for me to say yes.

A regular chat may not be able to open the GitHub link; that's fine—the prompt still works. In Claude Code or Codex, it reads the kit and uses the automatic checks.

The proof

We tested it: the made-up question was rejected

In the biology example, we submitted two new questions about cellular respiration. One was correct. The other said glycolysis produces 38 ATP molecules, which the materials don't say.

  1. The valid question included a sentence from the notes:
    "Glycolysis: occurs in the cytoplasm."
  2. The made-up question cited a sentence that wasn't in the materials.
    The kit searched for it and couldn't find it.
  3. Nothing was saved.
    The kit rejected the entire file and said which question to fix.
  4. Then, a completed guide said "stroma" where the correct answer was "thylakoids."
    The kit flagged the answer, showed the sentence from the materials, and warned against using it to study before correcting it.

Common questions

Before you get started

Do I need to know how to code?

No. The mother in the story doesn't code. The chat and ready-to-use prompts are enough. Claude Code comes in when you want interactive features and automatic checks.

What if I don't know the subject?

That's what step 1 is for: AI teaches you first using the student's materials and checks that you understand.

Isn't that cheating?

No. The guide teaches and tests. The questions are for practice without looking, and the end goal is for the student to study independently.

Does it work for every subject?

It works for subjects with written materials, such as notes, workbooks, or books. The example is biology, but the same formula works for chemistry, history, or law.

How much does it cost?

The kit is free. You need an AI tool, and a free chat is enough to get started. In the story, the mother later switched to Claude Code, which is paid.

What about the student's data?

It stays on your computer, in a folder just for you. Nothing is uploaded to GitHub.
